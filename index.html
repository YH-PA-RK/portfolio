I am an undergraduate nursing student at Chung-Ang University, interested in the brain mechanisms that generate and regulate appetite and eating behavior. I hope to understand how biological, psychological, and environmental factors contribute to obesity, how anti-obesity medications work, and why treatment responses vary between individuals. Drawing on my nursing education and patient-facing clinical practicums, I want to build a foundation in human-subject research and contribute to evidence that can help people experiencing difficulties with appetite and weight management.

Research interests

  • Appetite & Eating Behavior
  • Obesity & Treatment Mechanisms
  • Individual Treatment Response
  • Clinical Data & Digital Interventions

Questions about appetite and obesity

I want to understand why appetite and eating behavior can be difficult to regulate, and how physical, psychological, genetic, environmental, and social factors interact in this process. I am also interested in how the characteristics of obesity and approaches to treatment differ across age groups.

My questions extend to the mechanisms of anti-obesity medications and the reasons people respond differently to the same treatment. My primary interest is research involving people. I would also welcome opportunities to learn how animal studies investigate mechanisms that are difficult to examine directly in humans.

Learning through patient communication

During clinical practicums in pediatric, thoracic surgery, and orthopedic wards and a geriatric care setting, I learned to listen to patients and families and adapt my communication to their needs. I valued learning about collaboration among healthcare professionals alongside nursing procedures.

Through repeated conversations during routine care, I built rapport with patients and sought to understand discomfort and concerns that were not immediately apparent from their records. These experiences made me interested in bringing attentive listening and careful information gathering to a clinical research setting. My case studies were nursing coursework; I have not yet participated in a formal research project.

Finding evidence in health data

In community health nursing coursework and practicum, I served as a team leader on two occasions, analyzing health problems and preparing reports. For an assignment examining a regional health plan, I used public statistics to analyze population structure and distribution, calculate relevant indicators, and present the findings in graphs. During the practicum, I developed an evidence base for prioritizing community health problems and planning a health promotion program.

These experiences sparked my interest in interpreting data and translating findings into practical improvements in health. I hope to learn how to organize and analyze clinical and behavioral data and evaluate research findings, beginning with supervised research tasks and the methods needed to carry them out responsibly.

From clinical experience to research

I hope to contribute to the collection and organization of foundational data that help researchers understand obesity, appetite regulation, and individual treatment responses. In the longer term, I am interested in exploring AI-supported digital interventions and applications for people experiencing obesity or difficulties regulating eating behavior.

After graduation, I hope to gain clinical experience as a nurse, initially combining clinical work and research as circumstances allow. I then intend to pursue master's and doctoral study and devote myself to research full time. My goal is to connect patients' experiences with research questions and help build evidence for better care.
